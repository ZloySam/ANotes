what it is

A notebook, not a service.

ANotes is a notes app for Android. You type a note or dictate it, and you can work on it with any local AI model on your phone — offline, privately, with no network needed.

Offline, by construction

Speech recognition, speech synthesis, storage and search all run on the phone's own processor. No account, no server, no analytics. The only time it needs a connection is to download a voice model or a local AI model, once.

The files are yours

Every note is a plain Markdown file with YAML front matter, sitting next to a SQLite index. The files are the source of truth.

Local AI, built in

ANotes stands for "Local Notebook with AI." A local AI model runs right on your phone and works with your notes — no internet, no server, nothing sent anywhere.

why it exists

The problem it's actually solving.

01
You talk faster than you type.

Dictate a note and get text back, with nothing sent off the phone to produce it.

02
Note apps have burned you before.

Every note is saved as a normal text file on your phone. Even if you delete ANotes one day, your notes stay, and any text app can open them.

03
Most voice apps send your voice to their servers.

Once it's there, you can't check whether it's kept or used to train their AI. ANotes has no account, no cloud sync*, and no analytics — your voice becomes text on your phone.

* Optional backup to your own Google Drive is coming, encrypted on the phone before it leaves.
04
Transcripts get words wrong.

The original recording is kept next to the transcript, so you can play back what you actually said — and it stays ready to be transcribed again as better speech models arrive.

05
Getting a note down has to beat deciding not to bother.

One tap on the home-screen widget and it starts recording in a second — no screen to open, and never a voice model to wait for.

privacy

An architecture, not a promise.

Notes never leave the phone because nothing in the app has a way to send them anywhere — no network code on that path, no account to sync to. There's no encryption claim here: your notes rely on your Android device's own security, the same as any other app-private storage. No account profiles, no usage-tracking SDKs.

Coming next: optional backup to your own Google Drive. Notes are encrypted on the phone with a passphrase only you know, before anything leaves it — so Google can't read them, and neither can anyone without that passphrase.

availability

Coming soon.

ANotes is in alpha testing right now. Leave your email and you'll get one message when it's out — or send us a question.

What happens to your email
  • It's used only to answer you and, if you ticked the box, to send one message when ANotes is ready. No newsletter; never sold or shared.
  • It's stored on a private server run by the developer, never in a code repository. A copy goes to the developer's private Telegram chat so it's seen quickly.
  • Your IP address is used only in memory, to limit how often the form can be sent. It isn't saved with your message.
  • The spam check is Cloudflare Turnstile, which processes some technical data from your browser (Cloudflare's privacy policy).
  • Release-notice addresses are deleted once that message is sent; messages when the conversation is over, and after 12 months at the latest. Encrypted server backups age out within 6 months.
  • To see or delete what's stored about you, send the form again from the same address and say so.
